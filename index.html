Professional thread

Design depth became engineering depth without replacing the design instinct.

My background runs through graphic design, multimedia production and teaching into public-service software, large planning interfaces and systems engineering. I still approach code as a design material: something that can clarify an idea, expose its limits and make an interaction feel inevitable rather than merely functional.

Historical public material documents frontend application work for Länsstyrelsen Skåne and earlier leadership in design, multimedia and university teaching. Public LinkedIn material also documents close collaboration around the rebuilding of IKEA Kitchen Planner and IKEA Planner tooling using React, TypeScript and accessibility-focused frontend engineering.

The progression matters more to me than a framework timeline. Each step added another way to test an idea: visual composition, interaction, user observation, state modeling, performance, deterministic simulation and automated verification. The goal is not to replace intuition with process, but to give intuition better instruments.

About

I am interested in systems that can surprise me without becoming arbitrary.

The work becomes interesting when a small decision changes a physical model, a hidden state becomes visible, a simulation teaches something unexpected, or a tool gives somebody a little more agency than they had before.

I move comfortably between product thinking, visual and interaction design, and implementation. I like minimal surfaces with deep capability, playful prototypes that reveal a better direction, and systems whose behavior can eventually be explained precisely.

The medium changes — media playback, games, vehicle telemetry, public-service software, developer environments, knowledge graphs or programmable input — but the pattern is consistent: curiosity opens the space, disciplined modeling keeps the result coherent, and automation protects enough time to keep experimenting.

Working principles

A disciplined process should create more room for wonder, not less.

01

Start with the interesting question.

Sketch it, prototype it, simulate it, put it on real hardware or deliberately push it too far. Early work should make discovery cheap enough that a surprising result can change the plan.

02

Turn experiments into evidence.

Instrument the behavior. Use deterministic seeds, visual laboratories, accessibility checks, performance traces and explicit domain models so decisions can be compared instead of defended by memory.

03

Automate what has earned permanence.

When an idea proves itself, encode the invariant in types, tests, CI and documentation. The machinery is there to prevent regressions and repetitive review — which leaves more human attention for the next creative problem.

Build things that reveal how they work.
